Improbable

The evening you were born, the earth held still a moment —
day and night weighed even, a rare and quiet balance,
as if the sky itself paused just to let you enter,
though nothing marked the hour. No signal. No fanfare.

Eight billion people share this one turning stone.
Most will never cross paths, never trade a single word —
and somewhere in that vast, indifferent arithmetic,
I was only ever meant to be one more stranger you'd forget.

But I wasn't. Not to me, at least. Somehow, out of the odds,
I got to know you — however briefly, however far it's stayed since.
That was never something I was owed, or something I could keep,
just something rare enough that I'm still quietly glad for.

Thank you.

If this were an ordinary letter, I'd write it the way people usually do — "hope your year is wonderful," "you deserve the world," all the things that are true but say nothing. I wanted to write something that actually meant what it said.

So here's what I actually wish for you this year: that you get to be a little less hard on yourself. That the people around you see the version of you that I see — the one who notices things, who cares more than she lets on, who's funny in a way that sneaks up on people. I hope this year gives you room to actually enjoy your own life, not just get through it.

When I first built this page, you didn't know how I felt — so half of it was written in hints, meant to be found rather than said outright. You know now, so I won't pretend otherwise: I like you, more than I expected to. Ask anyone who's known me a while and they'll tell you I was the one rolling my eyes at all of this. I didn't think I'd lose sleep over one person. I did, more nights than I'd like to admit, over you.

I know where things stand, and I'm not writing this to change that. If I've learned anything, it's that caring about someone properly sometimes means wanting their happiness more than you want a particular outcome for yourself. Kahlil Gibran wrote once that love gives naught but itself and takes naught but from itself — that it doesn't ask to be possessed, or repaid, even in kind.

Some people find meaning in coincidences — matching numbers, aligned letters. I don't know if any of that really means anything. But if it does: six letters in my name, five in yours — however I arrange them, I've never once managed to spell anyone else's.

So happy birthday, Anuva. Whatever this page ends up meaning to you, I hope the wish underneath all of it lands the way it's meant to — that this year is genuinely good to you.
